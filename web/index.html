The problem

An approval outlives the app that asked for it.

Swaps, aggregators, payment and subscription apps ask for a delegate on your token account. The tab closes. The delegate stays, and it can move tokens without asking again until you revoke it.

When one of those apps is compromised, every wallet that approved it needs a revoke it can trust, fast.

Why Velon

Proof on both sides of your signature.

Executed before you sign

Velon runs the exact Revoke instruction itself, on the finalized bank it observed, with that cluster's activated features and the verified SPL Token program. A provider's simulation must reach the same account state.

Names the delegate

Who holds the permission, read from the chain: a wallet, a program's signer or a known program.

Fails closed

If anything disagrees, nothing is offered for signing. Some of the runtime's refusals:

  • feature unknown to the pinned runtime
  • simulation precedes observation
  • simulation/context slot mismatch
  • feature evidence outside observed bank

Read again after finality

"Revoked" appears only after the chain confirms the delegate is gone.

A receipt that replays without the chain

Every verified revoke keeps its evidence. Replay it later and the result still checks out.

How it works

Four steps. Two of them are proof.

  1. 01

    Observe

    Reads your token accounts from a finalized bank and lists every delegate, with how much it may spend.

  2. 02

    Execute

    Runs the revoke on that bank itself. A provider's simulation must reach the same account state.

  3. 03

    Sign

    Your wallet signs the exact bytes that were reviewed, and nothing else.

  4. 04

    Verify

    Waits for finality, reads the account again, confirms the delegate is gone and issues a verification ID.

Exploit response

A revoke people can trust when it matters.

When a protocol is compromised, each affected wallet sees the exact delegate, signs one reviewed revoke and gets a verification ID.
